concept

causeway is a generative music machine for norns whose whole point is to play everywhere at once. One slow, drifting harmonic stream is generated on the norns and fanned out simultaneously to the onboard PolySub engine, up to three external MIDI synths, a fourth MIDI port aimed at a DAW over the network, and a fifth voice sent as OSC to a second norns running the Strata sampler. The norns stops being a single instrument and becomes the conductor of whatever's wired to it.

The music is meant to move like water finding its way downhill — unhurried, gravitational, with memory. Nothing is sequenced note-for-note. Lines drift through a scale with directional momentum, occasionally leap, occasionally snap back to root, and the timing is deliberately loosened so it never sounds quantized. The name fits: a long, low, deliberate path across water.

causeway is a hub, not a soloist. The generative model is intentionally modest — scale, weighted drift, root gravity, a shared lead_last the harmony voices hang off of. The interesting part isn't the cleverness of any one line; it's that a single calm stream can light up the onboard pad, a rack of hardware, a DAW track, a sampler on a second norns, and a screen full of reactive visuals all at once.

five voices, one stream

polysub

pad

The only voice that sounds on the norns itself — slow chord clusters through PolySub. Picks a root from the middle third of the scale and stacks notes upward by twos with jitter; density (1–3) sets the stack height. It's the harmonic anchor.

midi

lead

The melodic core. Most steps walk one scale degree in the current direction. ~12% of the time it leaps two or three degrees, otherwise it reverses; flips direction near the range edges and never repeats a note twice. The pitch is built into a chord before sending.

midi

bass

A weighted walking line, grounded by gravity. 9% rest ("silence is part of the groove"), 28% root-gravity snap to the nearest root in range, otherwise a momentum walk with a 13% chance of dropping an octave. Density is a per-step fire probability.

midi

sec lead

A reactive voice with three modes that hang off the lead. harmony offsets the lead's last note by a fixed scale-step count · echo replays it after a delay in beats · counter drifts in the opposite direction. Voices at 80% of lead velocity to sit underneath.

midi · network

VST

Structurally identical to sec lead — same three modes — but on its own port and channel. Intended for a software synth in a DAW, reached through rtpMIDI as a virtual vport on the norns side.

osc

strata ↗

A sixth voice sent as raw OSC to a second norns running the Strata sampler. Its own random-walk generator and three modes: melodic (single notes), chordal (a chord stack), arp (the chord rolled out via nested clock.sync). Host IP is a hardcoded constant near the top of the file. The most recent body of work, merged 2026-05-26.

playing everywhere at once

The defining structural choice is that there is no single output. Each voice runs as its own independent clock.run loop, syncing to the norns clock at its own bar-rate; they share a key, scale, and octave window but make their own decisions.

Every destination keeps its own active-notes table so note-offs always land, and all_notes_off sweeps engine, all four MIDI ports, and Strata whenever playback stops or the script cleans up — so nothing hangs. Ableton Link transport is honored: a Link-connected DAW can start/stop causeway, and on start it waits for the next 4-bar boundary so it falls into the grid rather than jumping in mid-measure.

timing — drunk & syncopated

Two layers loosen the grid, applied before the lead, bass, and Strata steps — never the pad, which stays on its slow grid as the harmonic anchor.

layerbehavior
drunkscales a clock.sleep by the drunk amount, adding up to ~200ms of random delay so onsets scatter slightly behind the beat
syncopationsoft / medium / hard — probabilistically shifts a note onto the off-beat (a half-beat shift mostly, hard occasionally a full beat); probabilities climb 25% / 50% / 75%

wow / flutter

A tape-style pitch-instability layer runs in fx_loop at ~60fps and applies only to the PolySub pad — the MIDI voices are untouched. wow is a slow, mean-reverting Brownian wander; flutter is faster sample-and-hold variation. The two combine into a semitone offset re-applied to every sounding pad voice by calling engine.start again — updating pitch through PolySub's built-in lag without retriggering the amplitude envelope. Flutter also lightly amplitude-modulates the pad.

It's the one effect with its own front-panel UI: two small W / F bars at the bottom of the screen, brightening with numeric percentages while K3 is held.

seventeen audioreactive themes

The norns screen here is a visualizer, not a control surface. Seventeen themes, cycled live by holding K1 and turning E1, redraw on their own 1/20s metro independent of the musical clock. Every theme reads two shared signals: audio_level (an asymmetric envelope follow of the pad output — fast attack, slow release) scales brightness and motion, while on_note_trigger — fired by every voice on every note — dispatches to the current theme's trigger hook. The screen is always moving because the music is.

Everything else — per-voice devices, channels, octaves, rates, note lengths, velocities, densities, modes, the Strata voice, drunk and syncopation, and the optional DAYDREAM feed — is set in params before you start.

daydream — the osc sidecar

A second, optional OSC stream (the DAYDREAM params group, off by default) emits to a separate host/port from fx_loop at ~15Hz, aimed at driving an external generative-video / diffusion "scope." It sends continuous control values derived from the music — a noise scale from wow, an attention-bias term that falls as audio rises, a context scale from the active-voice count — plus play/pause and cache-reset events, and, keyed off the current root, a text prompt from a built-in table of twelve atmospheric scene descriptions ("iron bridge in fog, rust and rain"; "winter morning, bare trees, frost"). It's a one-way feed; causeway doesn't read anything back.

devlog

2026-03-27 — v1.0

Initial commit. causeway lands as a slow generative engine — pad on PolySub plus lead / bass / secondary MIDI voices, the drift-and-gravity note model, drunk timing, wow/flutter on the pad, and a small set of audioreactive screen themes. The header words the intent plainly: slow generative music for multiple synths.

2026-05-24 — the device build, synced back

A long gap, then the version that had grown on the device was synced into the repo — a much larger build than v1.0. This is where the screen grows from a handful of themes to seventeen, the timing gains syncopation alongside drunk, and the VST voice rounds out the MIDI fan-out.

The same day, work began on the strata-osc-voice branch: an OSC config and send helpers, then an independent generative voice (melodic / chordal / arp), then the PARAMS group and lifecycle wiring (voice count, all-off, clock), then a gate so the voice is inert when switched off.

2026-05-26 — the Strata bridge merges

Causeway → Strata OSC bridge merged to main. causeway can now conduct a second norns: it speaks /strata/noteon, /strata/noteoff, and /strata/alloff to Strata's matron OSC port. A small fix that day pointed the OSC host at White norns's then-current IP — a reminder that the destination address is a hardcoded constant that drifts with DHCP.

With the bridge in, causeway hits its current shape: one calm stream lighting up the onboard pad, a rack of hardware, a DAW, a sampler on another norns, and seventeen reactive visuals — a piece nobody is playing by hand.
